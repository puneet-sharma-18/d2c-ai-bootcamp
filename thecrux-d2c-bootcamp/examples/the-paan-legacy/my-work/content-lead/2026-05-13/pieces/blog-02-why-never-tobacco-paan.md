## Title
Why we will never sell tobacco paan. The five things we lost by saying no, and the one thing we gained.

## Subtitle
A founder's note on the easiest revenue line we will not cross.

---

The most common pitch I get from new investors goes like this. "Your tobacco-free paan is a beautiful niche. Now, when do you launch the 'traditional' line with a tobacco SKU? That is where the volume is. That is where the unit economics work."

The answer is no. The longer answer is what this post is about.

Tobacco paan, by the loose paan-wala economics, is somewhere around a ₹{20,000} crore informal market in India. It is sold at every paan stall, every railway station, every dhaba parking lot. It is the default. It is the volume. And we have decided that this brand will never sell it.

Here are the five things we lost by saying no, and the one thing we gained.

**One. We lost the largest single SKU category in the paan market.**

If we sold a "traditional" tobacco SKU at ₹{99} or ₹{149}, we would 4x our order volume in the first quarter. I know this because I have done the math three times in three different states of frustration over the last 18 months. The pull is real. The numbers are real. The pull is also a one-way door. Once we are a tobacco brand, we are a tobacco brand. The mothers and HRs and grandmothers who buy from us today will not buy from a tobacco brand. The volume is real and the cost of the volume is the brand. We picked the brand.

**Two. We lost the paan-wala customer.**

The man who eats two paans a day at the stand outside his office is not our customer. He never will be. He eats paan as a tobacco delivery vehicle and a five-rupee social break. He is not going to switch to a ₹{179} eat-and-swallow paan with rose petals in it. We accept that.

**Three. We lost the "authentic / traditional" SEO traffic.**

When someone in Tier-2 India searches for "traditional paan online", what they often mean is the tobacco-supari version their father chewed. Our SEO does not show up for that query, and we do not optimise for it. We optimise for "tobacco-free paan", "paan gift box", "eat-and-swallow paan". Lower volume. Higher intent. Different customer.

**Four. We lost the easy "we are a paan brand" shorthand.**

When I tell someone we make paan, the next five seconds are always us correcting the assumption. "No, not the kind you spit. No tobacco. Yes you actually eat it." Every conversation. Every founder-event introduction. Every investor pitch. We could shortcut this by being a generic paan brand. We do not, because the correction is the brand.

**Five. We lost some of our parents' generation.**

The 70-plus generation in many households still associates "paan" with the tobacco version. Some of them do not understand why we do not have a "regular" option. Some of them ask, politely, where the supari is. We tell them the answer politely. We lose a few of them. The ones we win are the grandmothers like Saroj, who never used tobacco in their paan in the first place, and who are simply delighted to find the thing they used to make at home now in a box.

Now, the one thing we gained.

**We gained a brand that the woman of the house chooses without hesitation.**

The single most consistent signal in our data is that the buying decision in our customer households is made by the woman. Often the wife. Sometimes the daughter. Occasionally the mother. The reason is not complicated. A tobacco paan is something you keep away from the kids and away from the carpet. An eat-and-swallow paan is something you serve after Sunday lunch and not think about again. The woman of the house has been refusing the tobacco version of this ritual for two generations. We are the option she has been waiting for.

Last month we read 94 customer messages. 22% of them used the phrase "tastes like my grandmother's paan". Another 13% said "first paan I have eaten without spitting". 15% were gifting-related, sent by HR teams and family-event planners who would not consider gifting a tobacco-paan box. That is not a coincidence. That is the brand. And every single one of those 94 messages comes from a decision we made on Day 1 to never put tobacco in a box with our name on it.

A tobacco SKU would have brought us volume. The decision to not have one brought us the customer we wanted.

If you have read this far, you are probably either someone who eats paan and stopped, or someone who buys from us and is reading this on the company-blog page. Either way, thank you. The next box we ship to you will not have tobacco in it. The one after that will not either. We are not going to revisit this decision.

Puneet
Founder, The Paan Legacy

P.S. If anyone tries to tell you tobacco paan is more traditional, ask them what their grandmother's paan had in it. Mine had almond, rose, gulkand, fennel and cardamom. No tobacco. No supari. No spitting. The traditional version is the one we are selling. The other version is the version the last 100 years sold us.
