# Founder note — why no tobacco, no supari, no spitting

> Puneet's recurring POV piece. Runs on the blog and on LinkedIn, rewritten quarterly. Lower-cap, conversational, founder voice.

---

People ask me, sometimes politely and sometimes not, why we do not make a "traditional" paan with tobacco. The honest answer is that we do make a traditional paan. The version that came before the tobacco.

Paan was a 2,000-year-old ritual before it became a tobacco vehicle. It was a digestive. It was a breath cleanser. The post-meal small luxury for a house that just ate well. The leaf was the wrap, not the message. Somewhere in the last fifty years, the leaf became a delivery system for a stimulant, the spitting became a public-health story and a category that used to be served at weddings became a stain on a wall in a stairwell.

I started The Paan Legacy because the paan my grandmother made on Sundays was none of those things. It was rose, gulkand, fennel, cardamom. It was almond on the special days, saffron on the festivals. It was eaten. The leaf went down with the rest. The next morning, no one had to clean anything.

So we make that paan. We hand-roll. We list the ingredients on the box. We refuse to make a "traditional" SKU with supari because the word traditional should not have to carry the cost of what the category did to itself in the last fifty years.

If you have not eaten paan in fifteen years because the last one you had was the wrong version, this is the one to start with. The Meetha Paan, ₹179. Eat one. Do not spit.

— Puneet
