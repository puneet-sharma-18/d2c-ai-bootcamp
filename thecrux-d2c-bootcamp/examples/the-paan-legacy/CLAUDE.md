## Section 3. Story

**Why this brand exists** (one paragraph, in my voice):

Paan is one of the oldest after-meal rituals in this country. It was always supposed to be a digestive, a breath cleanser, a small luxury after a heavy meal. Somewhere along the way it became a tobacco vehicle and a stain on the wall. The category traded a 2,000-year-old tradition for a quick hit and a road safety problem. I started The Paan Legacy because the paan my grandmother made on Sundays was none of those things. It was almond, rose, fennel, gulkand, occasionally a thread of saffron. It was eaten, swallowed, and remembered. We brought that paan back, in a package, without tobacco, without supari, without the things that turned the category into what it is today. We added a few of our own. Granola Goodness is not something my grandmother made. But she would have liked it.

**What we will never do** (anti-positioning):

- We never use tobacco, supari, or zarda. Not even in a "traditional" SKU. Not negotiable.
- We never make a spit-out paan. Every SKU is meant to be swallowed.
- We never claim "ayurvedic medicine" or any medical benefit beyond "may aid digestion". FSSAI's claim line is a wall, not a suggestion.
- We never use the words "gourmet" or "artisanal" in copy — the category has cheapened them. We use specific words: "hand-roasted", "small batch", "named ingredients".
- We never compete on price. Loose paan from a paan-wala costs ₹15. We are 10x that. The premium has to be felt in the experience and the ingredients, not argued in a discount.

## Section 5. Customer

- **Primary persona — Family ritual buyer**: Tier-1 / Tier-2 metro family, 32 to 48, household income ₹15L+, buys monthly. The "Sunday lunch finishes with The Paan Legacy" ritual. Often the woman of the house makes the buying decision, the husband or in-laws are the first to comment if quality slips.
- **Secondary persona — Gifting buyer**: Office HR or family-event organiser, age 28 to 45, buying in bulk (10 to 100 boxes) for Diwali, weddings, Eid, corporate Diwali bonus. Discovered us via a colleague's home receipt or a Diwali gift they received last year. Repeat seasonally, not monthly.
- **Tertiary persona — Curious health-modern**: 28 to 36, urban, single or DINK couple, started with Granola Goodness or Herbal Mukhwas, ramps into Meetha Paan or Almond Delight over 60 days. Drives Q1/Q2 growth before festival season takes over.
- **How they discover us**: Instagram (35%, gifting + ritual content), Zomato / Swiggy listings (25%, dessert section browse), word-of-mouth from a gift box (20%, "received this, loved it"), corporate gifting referrals (10%), founder press / YouTube (10%).
- **Why they stay**: A specific SKU clicked. The packaging is good enough to gift unchanged. The taste matches the heritage promise without the mess. They re-buy for the next family gathering, not for daily use.

### Brand voice rules

- **Always**:
  - "Hand-roasted"
  - "Small batch"
  - "Eat, do not spit"
  - "Tobacco-free"
  - "Tradition meets modernity"
  - "Named ingredients"

- **Never**:
  - "Gourmet" (category-cheapened)
  - "Artisanal" (same)
  - "Ayurvedic medicine", "cures", "treats" (FSSAI claim wall)
  - "Premium" (price-race trap)
  - "Authentic" (over-claimed across the category)
  - "Mouth freshener" (technically correct but cheap-feeling)
  - "Elevate", "unlock", "in today's fast-paced world", "game-changer" (AI / corporate tells)

- **Reading age**: Class 8 in Hindi-English Indian register. Short sentences. The grandmother is reading this, and so is the corporate HR.

### Compliance and safety

- **Regulated claims** (need substantiation before going into any copy):
  - "Aids digestion" — needs a published source (Ayush ministry monograph, FSSAI-recognised study). Safer wording: "traditionally used after meals".
  - "Tobacco-free" — must be verifiable. We always carry the FSSAI license number on every SKU page.
  - "Kid-friendly" — does NOT mean infant; specify age 6+ in any copy.
  - "Diabetes-friendly" — NO. Not approved by FSSAI for this product category.
  - Specific health claims ("good for gut", "helps acidity") — soften to "traditionally taken to support digestion" with no medical promise.

- **Restricted words** (no copy uses these without legal review):
  - "Medicinal", "treatment", "cure" (drug classification trigger under FSSAI)
  - "Sugar-free" without exact mg disclosure
  - "Diabetic-friendly", "weight-loss"
  - Health-condition naming ("acidity", "GERD") — keep to "digestion" language

- **Allergens** must be listed prominently on every product card and PDP: tree nuts (almond, cashew), dairy (in some SKUs), seeds (sesame, fennel).

- **FSSAI license number**: visible on every SKU listing and every order shipped. Manufacturing license number tracked separately in Ops Manager kit.
