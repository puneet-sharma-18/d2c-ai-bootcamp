## Section 3. Story

### Why this brand exists

I had a kid. I read the ingredient list on three "natural" baby brands at the shelf and could not pronounce half of what was on them. The half I could pronounce, I did not want on a newborn's skin. Then I called my paediatrician friend, who said the same thing back to me with citations. We started Little Lab because the category sells "natural" and "pure" as marketing words while burying paraffin, parabens and synthetic fragrance in the back of the bottle.

We are not a clean-label brand because clean-label is also a marketing word. We are a brand that prints the full ingredient list on the front of the bottle, top to bottom, with the active percentage where the regulator lets us state it. The paediatrician reviews every formula before it ships. The paediatrician reads every PDP before we publish it. That is the whole pitch. We do not need a slogan to dress it up.

### What we will never do

- Never claim "100% safe". Nothing is. CDSCO does not let you, and even if they did, no paediatrician would.
- Never use the word "natural". Too vague. The category has poisoned it.
- Never use "toxin-free". The word is unscientific, and a specific competitor owns the framing. We do not need it.
- Never use "gentle" as a stand-alone claim. Every brand says it. We say what is in the bottle instead.
- Never compete on price. The race to the bottom takes ingredient quality with it.
- Never use stock baby photos. We use real customers' kids, with consent, first names only.
- Never make a curative claim ("treats eczema", "cures cradle cap"). The category goes to jail for that. We say "for dry, irritated skin" and link the paediatric review.

## Section 5. Customer

- **Primary persona**: Tier-1 metro mom, 28 to 38, dual income, Mumbai / Bengaluru / Delhi / Pune, household income ₹15L+, reads ingredient lists before buying, follows three to five clean-label Instagram accounts. Buys for her own newborn or toddler. Will pay ₹500+ for a lotion if she trusts the brand. The "parent who reads the back of the bottle" line was written for her.
- **Secondary persona**: Paediatrician-referred parent. Bought Little Lab because their doctor recommended Cradle Cap Balm specifically by name. High trust on entry, ramps quickly into the daily lotion and massage oil over 60 days. ~25% of new D2C customers cite a paediatrician in the post-purchase survey.
- **Tertiary persona**: Tier-2 mom moving up from Mamaearth or Mother Sparsh because the brand promise stopped landing. Slower convert (2 to 3 visits before first purchase), strong on price sensitivity, weakest on tolerance for delivery delays.
- **How they discover us**:
  - Instagram founder content (~40%). The "I read the back of three baby lotions and could not pronounce half" reel is the recurring acquisition piece.
  - Paediatrician recommendation (~25%). Concentrated in Bandra, Indiranagar, Gurgaon clinics. The "doctor said this by name" thread shows up unprompted in reviews.
  - Mom WhatsApp groups in housing societies (~20%). Society-level word of mouth on cradle cap specifically.
  - Amazon search for "cradle cap" or "newborn lotion no fragrance" (~15%). Marketplace acquisition runs cheaper than D2C paid but the customer is less loyal.
- **Why they stay**: One specific SKU solved a specific problem (cradle cap, dry patches, bath time tantrums). They trusted us with the next SKU because the first one delivered. The full ingredient list on the bottle is what closes the trust loop. The Cradle Cap Balm cohort moves to the Newborn Daily Lotion within 45 to 60 days; that handoff is the single highest-leverage retention moment.

### Brand voice rules

**Always** (verbatim phrases, every teammate may quote these directly):

- "For the parent who reads the back of the bottle."
- "Ingredient list, top to bottom."
- "Paediatrically reviewed." (not "tested"; "tested" implies a clinical trial we did not run)
- "Small batch."
- "No fragrance, no fillers."
- "We print the active and the percentage."
- "We say what is in the bottle."

**Never**:

- "100% safe" (regulator + paediatrician both flag this)
- "Natural" (too vague, category has ruined it)
- "Pure" (over-claimed)
- "Gentle" without proof (cliche, also a CDSCO grey zone)
- "Toxin-free" (competitor framing, unscientific)
- "Premium" (price-race trap)
- "Authentic" (over-claimed across the category)
- "Elevate", "unlock", "harness", "leverage" as verbs (AI tells, fails brand sniff test)
- "In today's fast-paced world", "in the age of AI", "in an era where" (AI / LinkedIn tells)
- "It's not X. It's Y." / rhetorical-question hooks ("The result?", "Plot twist:")
- Curative or therapeutic claims ("treats", "cures", "prevents") without paediatric review citation
- Em dashes, en dashes, Oxford commas (founder house style, global)

**Reading age**: Class 8. Parents read this between feeds. Short sentences. Indian English with Hindi loanwords (mukhwas-style register does not apply here, but baby-care terms like "nappy", "cradle cap", "colic" are used naturally, no italics).

**Posture**: Technical, warm, no-nonsense. Say no in plain words when a product does not fit. Admit weakness without spinning (the SLS-no-foam thing, the Tier-2 delivery thing, the price objection). Founder signs personally where stakes are real (Riya). Sentence case for site copy. Priya in support answers fast, founder-first when the stakes are real.

### Compliance and safety

- **Regulated claims** (need substantiation before going into any copy):
  - **"Dermatologically reviewed"**: needs a named paediatrician panel sign-off on file (panel led by Dr. Mehta at Hinduja, Mumbai).
  - **"Hypoallergenic"**: needs patch-test data from {TestLab Mumbai, Report #}. Do not use without it.
  - **"Eczema-friendly"**: needs paediatric panel sign-off plus the specific SKU on the approved list. Cradle Cap Balm and Night-time Balm are cleared; the rest are not.
  - **"Safe for newborns"**: only on SKUs paediatrically reviewed for that age band, with the report ID in the listing.
  - Any % claim ("x% reduction in dryness"): needs the methodology referenced inline.
- **Restricted words** (no copy uses these without legal review):
  - "Anti-bacterial" (CDSCO drug-vs-cosmetic line)
  - "Medicinal", "therapeutic", "treatment", "cure", "prevents" (drug classification trigger)
  - Specific medical conditions ("eczema", "atopic dermatitis", "psoriasis") without a paediatric panel citation; default to "dry, irritated skin".
  - "Hypoallergenic", "dermatologically tested" (used precisely or not at all, see above).
- **Allergen disclosure**: every PDP and pack lists fragrance status, surfactant family, tree-nut presence (almond oil in the Massage Oil), oat presence (none currently, flag if formulation changes).
- **CDSCO**: cosmetic registration number on file for every SKU. Manufacturing license tracked by Ops Manager.
