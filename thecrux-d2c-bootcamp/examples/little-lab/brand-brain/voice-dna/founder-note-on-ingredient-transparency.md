# Founder note, why the ingredient list is on the front of the bottle

> Riya's recurring POV piece. Runs on the blog and on LinkedIn, rewritten quarterly. Lower-cap, conversational, founder voice.

---

A parent asked me at a clinic last month why we print the full ingredient list on the front of the bottle when nobody else does. The honest answer is, that is the brand. There is no second answer.

When I had my first kid, I bought a baby lotion that said "natural" on the front. I turned the bottle around and read fourteen ingredients. Six of them I could not pronounce. Two of them I knew were not natural by any definition the regulator uses. The word "natural" did all the work on the front. The list on the back did the opposite.

I called my friend who is a paediatrician. She said the same thing back to me with citations. So we built Little Lab. The first decision we made was that the list goes on the front. The active for Cradle Cap Balm is piroctone olamine at 1% w/w. The word piroctone olamine is on the front of the jar. The number 1% is on the front of the jar. If a parent does not know what piroctone olamine is, they can scan the QR code and the paediatrician panel explains it in two paragraphs. We trust the parent to do that.

We will never call a product "natural" because the word is too vague to mean anything on a newborn's skin. We will never call a product "toxin-free" because the regulator does not recognise the word and we are not in the business of running a marketing claim we cannot defend. We will never call a product "gentle" without saying what makes it gentle. The "what" is the list. The list is on the front.

If you have stopped trusting the baby category because the back of the bottle never matches the front, this is the brand to start with. The Newborn Daily Lotion, ₹549. Read the list. Decide for yourself.

— Riya
