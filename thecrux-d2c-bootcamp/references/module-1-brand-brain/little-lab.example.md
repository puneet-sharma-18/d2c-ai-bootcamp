## Section 3. Story

**Why this brand exists** (one paragraph, in my voice):

I had a kid. I read the ingredient list on three "natural" baby brands and could not pronounce half of them, and the half I could pronounce had no business being on a newborn's skin. Then I called a friend who is a paediatrician, who said the same thing back to me with technical citations. We started Little Lab because the category sells "natural" and "pure" as marketing words while burying paraffin and parabens in the back of the bottle. We are not a clean-label brand because clean-label is also a marketing word. We are a brand that prints the full ingredient list, top to bottom, with proportions where the regulator allows it. That is the whole pitch.

**What we will never do** (anti-positioning):

- We never claim "100% safe". Nothing is. CDSCO does not let you, and even if they did, no paediatrician would.
- We never use the word "natural". Too vague. The category has poisoned it.
- We never compete on price. The race to the bottom takes ingredient quality with it.
- We never use stock baby photos. We use real customers' kids, with consent, with their first names only.

### Brand voice rules

- **Always**:
  - "Ingredient list, top to bottom"
  - "Paediatrically reviewed" (not "tested", "tested" implies a clinical trial we did not run)
  - "For the parent who reads the back of the bottle"
  - "Small batch"
  - "No fragrance, no fillers"

- **Never**:
  - "100% safe" (regulator + paediatrician both flag this)
  - "Natural" (too vague, category has ruined it)
  - "Pure" (over-claimed)
  - "Gentle" without proof (cliche, also a CDSCO grey zone)
  - "Premium" (price race trap)
  - "Elevate", "unlock", "in today's fast-paced world", "game-changer" (AI / corporate tells)
  - "Toxin-free" (a specific competitor's term, also unscientific)

- **Reading age**: Class 8. Parents read this between feeds. Short sentences.

### Compliance and safety

- **Regulated claims** (need substantiation before going into any copy):
  - "Dermatologically tested" — needs third-party lab report from {TestLab Mumbai, Report #} 
  - "Hypoallergenic" — needs patch-test data
  - "Eczema-friendly" — needs paediatrician panel sign-off
  - "Safe for newborns" — only on SKUs paediatrically reviewed for that age band, with the report ID in the listing
  - Any % claim ("x% reduction in dryness") — needs the test methodology referenced

- **Restricted words** (no copy uses these without legal review):
  - "Anti-bacterial" (CDSCO drug-vs-cosmetic line)
  - "Medicinal" (drug classification trigger)
  - "Treats", "cures", "prevents" (drug claim)
  - Specific medical conditions ("eczema", "atopic dermatitis") — use "dry, irritated skin" instead unless you have the substantiation
